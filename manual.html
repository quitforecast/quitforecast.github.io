Today and the forecast

Today is the clock and the road: how long you have been clean, what your body is doing right now, and what is coming next.

The counter

Your clean time, counting up from your quit moment. Its format is yours to choose in Settings, and the same clock drives the widgets and the Watch.

The recovery timeline

Every stage of your body's recovery, cited to published research. Each stage carries three beats: what is healing, what you might feel, and what helps now. The stage you are standing on is marked, and stages you have passed on an earlier run say so.

The craving histogram

Your logged cravings by hour of day, drawn beside the counter once four are logged. It shows your own hours, never a guess about you.

The morning note

Some mornings, one line at the top of Today saying something your log has newly learned. It speaks only when the answer changed, never repeats a card already on screen, and hedges with "so far", because your log is the experiment.

The evening review

One question, once a day if you turn its reminder on: any cravings today? A "no" is the first true zero the forecast can count; a skipped evening is a blank, never a broken anything.

Celebrations

Each milestone celebrates once, when your body actually reaches it. A slip moves the clock and keeps the history: your longest run and your attempt count stay yours.

The SOS button

Always in the corner. It opens the craving toolkit, and it is the one control that never moves.

The log and patterns

The Log tab is where your own evidence accumulates: what set cravings off, when they hit, who you were with, and what all of it adds up to.

Logging a craving

Two taps and done. Triggers can stack, because cravings are compound. Strength and company are optional questions: a one-press log from the widget, the wrist or Control Center files no number at all, because the app never invents one you did not choose.

The trigger chart

Your triggers ranked by how hard they hit, weighted by strength, so the heavier fight leads even when it fires less often. The company card beside it counts who you were with, in plain counts.

The week's shape

Cravings by weekday, drawn once eight are logged. The sentence above it names your heaviest day only when one day truly carries the week; an even week is shown, not claimed.

The risk window

Once your log holds enough evidence, the app names the two hours your cravings cluster in, and can check in just before them if you ask it to. It speaks in plain counts from your own log, never in odds.

Strength over time

Average craving strength, this week against the weeks before. Cravings getting weaker is the most motivating fact a log can hold, and this is where yours shows it.

The quit-medicine record

If you use a patch, gum or another over-the-counter aid, the Log keeps your record of what you took and when you stepped down. It records, and it never advises.

The SOS toolkit

A craving crests and breaks in a few minutes. The toolkit exists to get you through exactly those minutes, with a shared five minute wave clock across every tool.

Breathe

The box breath: four in, four hold, four out, four hold, with the screen kept awake for the whole session.

Body scan

Tap where the craving lives, describe it, and watch it for thirty seconds. Watching a craving instead of obeying it is the whole technique, and it is cited on the screen that teaches it.

The games

Snake and a maze chase, because occupying the mind's visual workshop measurably blunts a craving. Best scores persist, and the wave clock runs through them.

The Big questions

One honest question at a time, for the cravings that are really about something else.

The why

Seven short cards on why humans reach for these substances at all: the wanting machinery, the after-work reach, the cue, the evolutionary story. Every card is cited, and each visit opens on a different one.

Crisis and support lines

Real, staffed lines, one tap from the SOS screen and from Settings; 988 and the SAMHSA helpline answer day and night. An app can ride a wave with you. Some nights need a person.

The crisis lines page, one tap away.

Rebuild, moves and badges

Quitting leaves a gap where the habit lived. Rebuild is the catalogue of what goes in its place, and the shelf that remembers what you have already built.

The catalogue

Moves with their research named where it exists. Each states why it works, the steps, and the obstacles you will actually meet. The ones without citations say Experiment, plainly. Add what fits, set your own cadence, mark it done when it is.

Your list and the footer

Your chosen moves lead the screen; the footer counts what is done, earned and set aside, and each count opens its own list.

The savings tin

The money the habit is not taking anymore, set aside toward something you name. The tin records what you put in; it never assumes you stayed quit.

Badges

Earned by what you do, never by the clock: a pattern found, ten waves ridden, a week of your own data, a slip told fast. Nothing on the shelf is ever taken back, because the learning a badge marks already happened. No points, no levels.

The community board

Moves submitted by people quitting, reviewed by a person before anything is public, voted on by everyone. Your private feedback never appears here; the board is its own door.

Watch, widgets and Control Center

The moments that matter rarely happen with the app open. Every surface below logs the same craving into the same record, one press, nothing asked.

The Watch app

Your counter, a one-tap craving log, and the SOS breath, on your wrist. After the tap, an optional sheet offers strength, trigger and company; skipping any of it costs nothing, and the craving is already logged either way. If you track more than one quit, the wrist switches between them without touching the phone.

Home Screen widgets

The counter ticking live, money saved, and the next milestone. Long-press to pin a widget to one habit; left alone it follows whichever the app has open.

Control Center and the Lock Screen

A log button that files a craving without opening the app, an SOS button that opens the app straight into the breath, and lock-screen accessories for the counter.

The Live Activity

Through the first three days, your counter and next milestone sit on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, and a tap lands on the SOS breath. It retires itself once the acute phase passes.

Siri

Say "Craving SOS in QuitForecast" and the breath opens directly, with the app closed, mid-craving, no hunting.

Health and baselines

The forecast says your body recovers. The Health tab is where your own Apple Health data gets to agree, against your own before.

The five metrics

Resting heart rate, heart rate variability, cardio fitness, respiratory rate and blood oxygen. Each is charted against your own pre-quit baseline, with what published research expects underneath.

The baseline

Your own average from before the quit, drawn only when enough pre-quit days exist to mean something. A baseline from two readings is noise, so the app waits rather than guesses.

What never leaves the phone

Health data is read on your device and never transmitted anywhere, never backed up by this app, and never quoted by any surface that leaves the phone. That is a promise the code holds by test.

Data, backup and privacy

No accounts, nothing to sign into, and your record is yours. This section is the manual for keeping it that way.

Where your data lives

On your phone, in one file the app owns. The optional iCloud backup carries exactly the fields written down in the app's own allowlist, and Health readings are never among them.

Export

One tap in Settings hands you everything the app knows as a readable file. Your data is yours; a copy should cost nothing.

Delete everything

The other one tap. It takes the habits, the log, the shelf, the backup flags, all of it, and it cannot be undone. Unsent feedback is discarded rather than delivered, because somebody erasing themselves does not want their last message posted afterwards.

Feedback, help and requests

Send feedback is anonymous and read by a person. Get help is a private thread a person answers, usually within a day. Feature requests are the public door: approved requests are visible to everyone and votes steer what gets built. Three doors, and the private two never feed the public one.

QuitForecast is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat anything. It holds no advice your own doctor should not overrule.
